Every one of those runs on the same thing

None of it happens without a specific, trusted dataset underneath that somebody owns and everybody else is willing to build on. Most companies have a name for those: data product. A useful idea attached to a very loose word.

Ask what they mean by it. To one customer it's a dashboard, to the next an entire domain's data, to a third a single KPI. All three will nod along and mean something different.
What makes one dataset a data product Now multiply that by every team in the org
The problem without dbt

Every one of those data products gets built by a different team, at a different technical level, with different tools. Keeping their definitions and transformations consistent, so leadership's dashboard actually adds up, is the hard part.

dbt Core solves the fundamentals

dbt Core brings software engineering best practices to analytics, and everything else follows from that. SQL is the standard, because it is the simplest and most widely understood language for working with data. Long scripts become modular models built out of each other rather than copied around, so logic is written once, reused everywhere, and dbt can work out how it all connects on its own. Documentation travels with the code, one to one: one SQL script, one model, one table in the warehouse, one place its description lives, right beside the thing it describes.

But scale creates new challenges

Core solves the fundamentals. But once a company has dozens of teams and hundreds of models built on it, a new layer of problems shows up.
